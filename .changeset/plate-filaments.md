Features: **Which filaments each plate uses.** A project can have more filaments than the U1 has toolheads, and until now nothing said which of them a plate needs. `inspect()` now reads it per plate: each filament the plate uses, how (its objects, a part, paint, a filament set for a range of heights, supports and raft, a tool change at a layer) and between which heights, with the most the plate needs at the same height. It counts what the slicer counts: a part's own filament or its object's, none of a part's own filament when it is painted all over, and tool changes at a layer only on a plate that otherwise prints with one filament. A conversion's notes say when a plate needs more filaments at the same height than the U1 prints with (it can't print as it is), when a plate uses more than four but never more than four at once (it prints with filament changes at pauses), and, for a project with more than four filaments, which ones each plate uses. The first two are things to act on before printing, so they come near the top of the notes under their own topic, `plate-filaments`, right after a filament the U1 can't take as it is, and at the top of `report.warnings`. Checked against Snapmaker Orca 2.4.1's own slices of converted projects: the same filaments, and the same number at one height, on every plate. The number is an estimate from the model's geometry, not a slice: a filament counts from the lowest to the highest point it is used at. Converted files don't change. Library: `inspection.plateFilaments` and `report.plateFilaments` (`PlateFilaments`, `PlateFilamentUse`, `FILAMENT_USES`). CLI: the notes on `convert`, and a line per plate under `--verbose`. A new example, `examples/plate-filaments`, prints a loading plan and exits 1 on a plate the U1 can't print as it is.
